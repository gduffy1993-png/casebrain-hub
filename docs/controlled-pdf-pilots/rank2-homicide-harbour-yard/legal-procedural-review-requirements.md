## Qualified criminal-law review

The qualified criminal practitioner must record answers and exact amendments for:

1. whether the D1 evidence supports a proposed murder charge, including the required intent and evidential sufficiency;
2. whether and how manslaughter should appear as an alternative to murder for D1—separate count, alternative verdict/routing, or not at all;
3. the legally correct manslaughter route, elements and particulars proposed for D2;
4. whether evidence supports D2 causal contribution and the identified unlawful/dangerous act;
5. whether secondary liability, joint enterprise or another participation route arises and, if so, whether it should be designed or expressly excluded;
6. joinder, count ordering, alternatives, potential duplicity and prejudice between the two defendants;
7. exact indictment wording and compliance with current CrimPR Part 10, Practice Directions and form;
8. legal causation, including multiple acts, vulnerability/medication, delayed deterioration and clinical treatment;
9. admissibility and permissible use of each defendant's interview and co-defendant material;
10. bad character, hearsay, admissions, agreed facts and any severance implications relevant to the designed sources;
11. proper PTPH/case-management steps, expert issue identification and timetable;
12. disclosure duties, defence statements, expert unused material and continuing review;
13. bail/remand and any custody-time-limit design if later included; and
14. whether any special consent, homicide-case handling/referral or victim/bereaved-family procedure must be represented.

## Admissions, agreed facts and causation review

The criminal practitioner and relevant experts must jointly confirm that the state model does not collapse:

| Object | States requiring independent source and date |
|---|---|
| Admission | Draft → proposed → accepted → signed → served/filed → admitted in evidence → varied/withdrawn/rejected |
| Agreed fact | Draft → proposed → accepted text → signed → filed → admitted in evidence → varied/withdrawn/disputed |
| Causation | Source observation/analytical result → factual contribution → medical cause → susceptibility/thin-skull evidence → alleged intervening act/treatment → expert opinion → operating/substantial-cause or other party submission → court ruling → verdict |

Specific review questions:

1. Is the D1 torch-possession proposition capable of formal admission, and is its deliberately narrow scope clear?
2. Which facts can realistically be agreed by both defendants and prosecution without resolving disputed acts, intent or causation?
3. How should co-defendant-specific admissions be presented and used?
4. What source proves each transition, and can a party withdraw or vary it at that stage?
5. Does the joint expert statement express only professional agreement/disagreement rather than legal causation?
6. Which causation questions are for expert evidence, submissions, judge and jury respectively?
7. How must operating/substantial-cause submissions, susceptibility/thin-skull evidence and any alleged intervening treatment be framed without AI deciding whether treatment, alcohol or anticoagulants break or preserve legal causation?

## Data, confidentiality and audience review

Before generation, confirm:

- fictional medical records are proportionate and minimised while remaining diagnostically useful;
- defence expert privilege/service state is correct and cannot leak to CPS/court before service;
- one defendant's instructions, admissions and private material cannot leak to the other defendant/client;
- bereaved-family and deceased-sensitive content is appropriate for each audience;
- expert/counsel extracts contain only authorised material;
- no real personal data, medical data, venue, case reference or confidential fact is used; and
- view, copy, export, API and composed prose share the same truth/state while applying audience restrictions.
